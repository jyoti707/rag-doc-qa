The Parliament of India
Source: Constitution of India (public document, legislative.gov.in / India Code)

This document gives a short overview of The Parliament of India.

The Parliament of India consists of the President, the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha. The Lok Sabha has a maximum strength of 552 members, of whom 543 are elected from territorial constituencies. The normal term of the Lok Sabha is five years unless it is dissolved earlier.

The Rajya Sabha has a maximum strength of 250 members, including 12 nominated by the President. The Rajya Sabha is a permanent House and cannot be dissolved, but one third of its members retire every two years. A Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, and the Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations on it.

The Speaker presides over the Lok Sabha. A minimum age of 25 years is required to contest for the Lok Sabha and 30 years for the Rajya Sabha.
