Article 21 and Judicial Interpretation

Source: Constitution of India (public document, legislative.gov.in / India Code)

This document gives a short overview of Article 21 and Judicial Interpretation.

Article 21 states that no person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law. In Maneka Gandhi v Union of India (1978) the Supreme Court held that the procedure must be fair, just and reasonable. The Supreme Court has read the right to live with human dignity into Article 21.

In K. S. Puttaswamy v Union of India (2017) a nine-judge bench held that privacy is a fundamental right under Article 21. Article 21 is available to citizens and non-citizens alike because it uses the word person. The Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 gives effect to Article 21A and came into force on 1 April 2010.

Courts have also linked Article 21 with the right to clean environment, speedy trial and free legal aid. Before 1978 the Court took a narrow view in A. K. Gopalan v State of Madras (1950), reading Article 21 as protection only against arbitrary executive action.
