# High Courts and Subordinate Courts

*Source: Constitution of India (public document, legislative.gov.in / India Code)*

This document gives a short overview of High Courts and Subordinate Courts.

## Key points

Every state has a High Court, although two or more states can share a common High Court. High Court judges retire at the age of 62. Article 226 empowers High Courts to issue writs for enforcing Fundamental Rights and for any other purpose.

Article 227 gives each High Court the power of superintendence over all courts and tribunals within its territory. The oldest High Courts, set up in 1862, are those at Calcutta, Bombay and Madras. The Governor appoints district judges in consultation with the High Court of the state.

The district court is the principal civil court of original jurisdiction in a district, while the Sessions Court tries serious criminal cases. Gram Nyayalayas were created under a 2008 Act to provide speedy and inexpensive justice in rural areas.
