Union-State Relations and Federalism
Source: Constitution of India (public document, legislative.gov.in / India Code)

This document gives a short overview of Union-State Relations and Federalism.

The Constitution divides legislative powers between the Union and the states through three lists in the Seventh Schedule. The Union List contains subjects such as defence, foreign affairs and atomic energy on which only Parliament can legislate. The State List includes subjects such as police, public health and agriculture on which state legislatures normally legislate.

The Concurrent List includes subjects such as education and forests on which both Parliament and state legislatures can make laws. If a state law on a Concurrent subject conflicts with a central law, the central law generally prevails under Article 254. Residuary powers over subjects not listed in any list belong to the Union under Article 248.

Article 280 provides for a Finance Commission to be constituted every five years to recommend sharing of taxes between the Union and states. The Goods and Services Tax Council, created under the 101st Amendment in 2016, brings the Union and states together for decisions on GST.
