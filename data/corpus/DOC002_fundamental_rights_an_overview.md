# Fundamental Rights: An Overview

*Source: Constitution of India (public document, legislative.gov.in / India Code)*

This document gives a short overview of Fundamental Rights: An Overview.

## Key points

Fundamental Rights are contained in Part III of the Constitution, covering Articles 12 to 35. The Constitution guarantees six Fundamental Rights: equality, freedom, protection against exploitation, freedom of religion, cultural and educational rights, and constitutional remedies. The Right to Property was originally a Fundamental Right under Article 31 but was removed by the 44th Amendment in 1978.

After that change, property became a legal right under Article 300A rather than a Fundamental Right. Fundamental Rights are justiciable, which means a person can go to court to enforce them. Article 12 defines the term State to include the Government and Parliament of India, state governments and local authorities.

Article 13 declares that any law inconsistent with Fundamental Rights is void to the extent of the inconsistency. Articles 14 and 21 are available to every person, whereas rights such as those in Article 19 are available only to citizens.
